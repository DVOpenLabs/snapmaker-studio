What to do

  1. Press Open a model (top of the sidebar, or the large button on Home), or drag an .stl or .3mf onto the Studio window.
  2. Studio opens the Workspace and reads the file locally.
  3. Read the status line, the verdict banner and What's in this design.

You are done when

  • You see the file name and Checked — here's what we found. Press Prepare U1 copy when you're set.
  • A verdict banner appears, for example Can prepare a U1 copy.
  • Your original file is untouched, and no prepared copy has been written.

If something is different

Nothing happens after choosing a file.

The file may be damaged, or it may not be an STL or 3MF file. Download it again. For a 3MF, open Compatibility, which should say “Studio could not open this file as a 3MF project.”

I do not have a project to try.

Use the example files in the examples folder (opens in a new tab). Open demo_offplate_foreign.3mf to follow along.

Safety

Opening a file never changes it. Studio is local-first: the file is read on your computer and nothing is uploaded.

What to do

  1. Open This print for the whole job, or Compatibility for the full list (the Open the full checks link takes you there).
  2. Read Object placement first. Studio can move this example onto the plate, and preparing a U1 copy also adjusts incompatible project settings.
  3. Skim the cards. For each, read the label, then its Do this: line. Open Technical detail only if you are curious.
  4. Make a short list: confirmed problems, things to check in Orca, things Studio cannot tell.

You are done when

  • You can say which findings are facts and which are gaps.
  • You know whether the next step is a fix in Studio (stage 3) or a job for Orca.

If something is different

The findings say “Invalid value for …”. Is my model broken?

Usually not. The text says the model may be fine and the project settings are for another printer.

Cards show “Checking…” for a while.

Studio first tries to reach a printer. When none answers, the cards switch to Printer not found and Studio can’t tell.

My findings differ from the example.

They describe your own file. The labels mean the same thing.

Studio can’t tell

“Studio can’t tell” means Studio has no way to read the fact from your printer. It does not mean your printer cannot do it. Studio can also say it when the file leaves a fact out.

Safety

The checks are read-only. Studio never edits your file while diagnosing it.

Misplaced, or too big to fit?

Three example projects, three different placement messages. Read what Studio shows, then decide what you would do. The numbers and wording are copied from Studio v1.5.0, run on the guide's example files.

Worked example (the interactive version needs JavaScript):

Case 1 — one small object, off to one side

  • Studio says: 1 object is outside the U1's printable area.
  • Object 2 · 20 × 20 × 20 mm — Hangs 55.0 mm past the right edge.
  • Studio offers a button: Move onto the plate (saves a copy).

What is the right next step?

  • Press Move onto the plate, then prepare the moved copy. Best answer. Right. The object is small and simply sits too far to the right. One shift of the whole layout brings it back onto the plate, and Studio re-checks the file it wrote. Your original is not touched.
  • Rescale the object in Snapmaker Orca. Not correct. Nothing here says the object is too big: it is 20 mm and the plate is far larger. Rescaling would change your model's real size for no reason.
  • Ignore it: 55 mm is not much. Not correct. Even a small overhang matters: part of the object would be off the plate. A small part can pass every size check and still land off the plate, which is why Studio names the edge and the distance.

Case 2 — one long bar that hangs off both ends

  • Object 1 · 320 × 20 × 20 mm — Hangs 1.0 mm past the left edge and 50.0 mm past the right edge.
  • Studio says: Moving the objects as one piece would not bring them all on, so Studio will not guess. Open the project in Snapmaker Orca and use Arrange.
  • There is no Move button.

Why is there no Move button, and what do you do?

  • The object overhangs both the left and right edges, so shifting it cannot make it fit. Studio does not rotate or resize it; open it in Orca. Best answer. Right. Overhang on two opposite edges plus a 320 mm length tells you no shift can fit the object as it is oriented, so the Move button would not help. Studio's own advice is to open the project in Snapmaker Orca; if the part really is longer than the plate you will also need to resize, rotate or split it there. Studio does not edit your model.
  • Look harder for the Move button; it is probably hidden. Not correct. There is none. Studio offers the move only when one shift brings every object on the plate. When it cannot, it says so instead of half-fixing the project.
  • Use Scale Doctor to shrink it. Reasonable, with a catch. Possible, if you really want a smaller part. Scale Doctor previews a uniform resize, and writes a scaled copy only for an STL; for a 3MF you resize in Orca. Shrinking changes what you print, so make it a deliberate choice, not a way to silence a warning.

Case 3 — two small cubes far apart

  • Object 2 · 10 × 10 × 10 mm — Hangs 35.0 mm past the right edge. (Object 1 is on the plate.)
  • Studio says the same thing as in case 2: Moving the objects as one piece would not bring them all on, so Studio will not guess.
  • There is no Move button.

Each cube is tiny. What is the real problem?

  • The objects are too far apart: together they are wider than the plate. One shift cannot bring both onto the plate, so rearrange them in Orca with Arrange. Best answer. Right. Neither cube is big; the layout is. Moving them as one piece would push one off the other side, so Studio stops. Arrange in Snapmaker Orca can bring the objects closer together.
  • The cube is too big for the U1. Not correct. The cube is 10 mm. The problem is the distance between the two objects, not the size of either one.
  • Press Move onto the plate anyway. Not correct. The button is not offered here, on purpose: moving the whole layout could not bring both cubes onto the plate.
  • What to take from it
  • Check the size of the object against the overhang: a small object that hangs off one edge is misplaced.
  • Overhang on two opposite edges means shifting alone cannot fit the object; it may need rotating, resizing or splitting.
  • If Studio offers Move onto the plate, its check found a single shift that fits. If it does not, read the placement card: either everything already fits, the layout needs changes in Orca, or it is a multi-plate project, which Studio never repositions.

Assigning a material is not loading it

You open a project with Project Materials. For slot 1 you pick a spool from your inventory and confirm an installed Orca preset, select Remember this mapping, and press Prepare with these choices. The copy is created and the mapping is saved. Select everything Studio did.

Worked example (the interactive version needs JavaScript):

  • Wrote the spool's color into the new copy. Yes, Studio does this. Yes. The review says Colour changed to Red: the chosen spool's color is written into the new copy.
  • Named the installed preset in the new copy. Yes, Studio does this. Yes. Studio writes the confirmed preset name into the copy. For a preset you made yourself, that does not prove Orca applies its temperature, flow or cooling; check those in Orca.
  • Saved the mapping on this computer, after the copy was written. Yes, Studio does this. Yes: in this example the mapping was saved after the copy was created. Saving can fail separately; Studio then warns that the copy was made but the mapping could not be saved. Saved mappings are checked again when reused.
  • Loaded that filament into a toolhead. No, Studio does not do this. No. Studio never loads, swaps or changes spools. Putting the right filament in the right toolhead is your job at the printer.
  • Changed the spool in your Spoolman, SpoolEase or Bambuddy inventory. No, Studio does not do this. No. Studio only reads a provider; it never writes to it.
  • Wrote the spool's ID or remaining weight into the project. No, Studio does not do this. No. The review says spool details are not read from the file and that Studio never writes them into the project.
  • What to take from it
  • Studio can put a color and a preset name into a new copy. It cannot put filament into a toolhead.
  • The U1 has four toolheads. Studio plans around them, and then you load the colors in the order the project lists them.
  • Your spool inventory is read, never changed.

What to do

  1. If Object placement offers Move onto the plate (saves a copy), press it. Studio writes …_placed_U1.3mf next to your original.
  2. Press Open a model and choose that moved copy. (The move and the U1 preparation are separate actions.)
  3. Leave Preserve creator settings selected unless you have a reason, then press Prepare U1 copy.
  4. Optional, if you track spools: on Compatibility, pick a spool and an installed preset for each color under Project materials, press Review & prepare, read the review, then Prepare with these choices.

You are done when

  • You see U1 copy created — review setup before slicing, followed by a note that your original file is not modified.
  • New files beside your original, for example …_placed_U1_SnapmakerU1.3mf. You may also see a .orig.3mf snapshot Studio keeps next to a prepared copy.
  • When you open the prepared copy, Object placement says the object sits inside the printable area.

If something is different

There is no Move button.

Read the placement message. Either every object already fits, no single shift brings them all on, or Studio will not move this project (it never repositions multi-plate projects). Work through the placement example above.

The prepared copy still shows warnings.

Studio prepares the file for the U1; it does not fix every setting. Read Your fix plan: each step is marked Do this in Studio or Do this in Orca.

Prepare stopped appears with Project Materials.

Two slots share one preset but the project declares different vendors or types for them. Choose a different preset for one slot; Studio does not edit the project for you.

Important

Move onto the plate only changes where objects sit. To get a U1 copy that is also on the plate, prepare the moved copy. Preparing the original on its own does not move anything.

Safety

Your original is never modified. Every action here writes a new file.

Read a preparation report

After preparing, Studio lists what it did. Sort these real lines from a prepared copy of the example project into the section they appear under, and see what each one asks of you.

Worked example (the interactive version needs JavaScript):

  • Printer identity changed to Snapmaker U1 → Adjusted for U1 project compatibility. Adjusted. Studio changed the printer identity to Snapmaker U1. That is why Orca can open the copy with U1 settings; the identity change alone does not check every setting.
  • 533 creator settings kept → Kept from the original file. Kept. These are the creator's own print settings, carried over because you chose Preserve creator settings. Kept means the value reached the copy; where Snapmaker Orca rebuilds or ignores a setting, the row says so; still glance at the printer and filament settings in Orca.
  • nozzle_type: stainless_steel → hardened_steel → Adjusted for U1 project compatibility. Adjusted. A before → after pair under Adjusted for U1 project compatibility is a real change Studio made. Skim Before and after for any line that surprises you.
  • 24 optional recommendations available, including tower placement and tower wall type → Optional recommendation, not applied. Not applied. Recommendations change nothing unless you choose them, for example with Prepare another copy with recommended settings.
  • Turn on supports — Do this in Orca → A step for you in Snapmaker Orca. Yours. Studio cannot choose supports for you; its fix plan marks this step Do this in Orca, so review it there before slicing.
  • What to take from it
  • Kept means the value reached the copy, not that Orca uses it: read any note on the row. Not applied recommendations stay unapplied until you choose them. Adjusted is worth a skim. Do this in Orca is your job.
  • Anything Studio could not verify is listed under What Studio could not check rather than assumed fine; compare it with your original in Orca and report anything unexplained.
  • Your original is never modified: Return to the original points Studio back at it.

What to do

  1. After preparing, scroll to What survived preparing this copy and note the counts.
  2. Expand What Studio changed (n), then What stayed the same (n).
  3. Open Changes Studio made and Before and after for the old and new values; long values are shortened on screen.
  4. Read Your fix plan: anything marked Do this in Orca is for the next stage.

You are done when

  • You can name what Studio changed and what it left to you.
  • You know where Return to the original is, and that the original was never changed.

What to do

  1. On the prepared result, press Open in Snapmaker Orca (or Copy path).
  2. In Orca, check: the printer is Snapmaker U1; the filaments and colors match what you will load; the objects are on the plate (use Arrange if unsure); supports are set the way you want.
  3. Slice in Orca (Slice plate).
  4. Export the G-code (the file of printer instructions) with Orca's own export command and note where it is saved. Menu wording can differ between Orca versions.
  5. Keep the exported .gcode. Save the Orca project too if you want your Orca settings kept.

You are done when

  • Orca opens your prepared copy, and Studio stays as it was: it does not control Orca and does not slice.
  • You have an exported .gcode file for the next stage.

If something is different

The button says Install Snapmaker Orca.

Install Orca from its releases page (opens in a new tab), then use Copy path and open the prepared file from inside Orca.

Studio says “Couldn’t launch Snapmaker Orca — open the file from Orca instead.”

Open the prepared file from inside Orca; Copy path helps you find it.

Studio says “Couldn’t find the prepared file — try preparing it again.”

The copy was moved or deleted. Prepare it again.

Important

The prepared copy is a starting point. Always review it in Orca before slicing: Studio's report from the previous stage lists what it changed.

Example — answers come from what Studio shows; nothing here touches your files or printer

Unknown, or a confirmed problem?

Studio is more sure about some findings on a sliced job than about others. These lines come from Studio's checks of two example files written for this guide: one whose header names a U1, one whose header names a Bambu Lab printer. Neither was checked against a connected printer. Decide how sure Studio actually is.

Worked example (the interactive version needs JavaScript):

  • The G-code header names a Bambu Lab printer. → Confirmed in the file. Confirmed in the file. Studio read the printer name in the G-code header; no printer needs to be connected for that. What follows from it is a separate finding: Studio does not recognize that name as the U1, so its card says Sliced for a different printer, marked “Worth settling first”, and its Evidence line says the name is not one Studio recognizes. Studio rates that mismatch likely, not confirmed, though the card does not print that rating. Action: re-slice the model in Snapmaker Orca with a U1 profile.
  • Tools this job needs — “Studio cannot confirm your printer has the toolheads this job expects.” → Unknown. Unknown. Studio would need information from your printer to know. This is not a problem found; connect the printer in Printer Hub, or check yourself.
  • Nozzle size — “Studio has no way to see which one is installed.” → Unknown. Unknown. Check the nozzle on the printer. You can also record what is fitted under Settings → Nozzles.
  • Studio cannot tie this job to your project — “nothing in either file identifies the model.” → Worth checking. Worth checking. Studio cannot tell whether this job was sliced from the project you checked, so it asks you to be sure you picked the right file.
  • Sliced for the machine Studio prepares for — “The job's commands were generated for a Snapmaker U1.” → Looks right. Looks right. This appears when the job's header names a U1. Still advice, not a promise that the print will succeed.
  • What to take from it
  • “Studio can’t tell” is not “incompatible”. It means Studio lacks information it needs, often because no printer is connected.
  • A problem is confirmed only when Studio has the evidence in hand: something read from the file (the printer named in the G-code); something your connected printer reported (its bed size or toolhead count); or something you confirmed yourself (a nozzle size). When the header names a printer Studio does not recognize, the mismatch is rated likely, not confirmed.
  • For unknowns, check the item yourself or connect the printer. For a confirmed problem, follow the card's action, then check the job again.

What to do

  1. Open After Slicing (Advanced sidebar, More tools, or stage 3 of This print).
  2. Optional: type the folder where Orca saves exports and press Watch this folder. Studio looks only while After Slicing or This print is open.
  3. Otherwise drag the .gcode onto the window or paste its full path under Open the G-code your slicer produced, then press Check this job.
  4. Read Ready to send? and then each finding. Sort them into confirmed, worth checking and unknown.
  5. Read What the printer will actually do and What to load (what each slot needs).

You are done when

  • You know which findings are facts, and which are gaps that still need checking.
  • You can say what you will do about each: re-slice, check yourself, or connect the printer.

If something is different

Nothing appears in the watched folder.

Check that it is the folder Orca exports to, and that After Slicing or This print is open. You can always paste the file's path.

Studio says “that file does not exist”.

Studio could not find a file at that path. Copy the full path of the exported .gcode again.

Many items say “Studio can’t check this”.

That is expected when no printer is connected. Connect the printer (see the task) or check those items yourself.

Studio can’t tell

On its own, Studio reads only the file. With no printer connected it cannot tell which tools are present, whether slots are loaded, whether the bed matches or which nozzle is fitted (unless you confirmed the nozzle yourself).

Safety

Studio reads and never changes your G-code, and it never sends anything on its own. Printing starts only when you start it on the printer or confirm it in Printer Hub.

Limit

Install and open Studio

Use this when: You have not installed Studio yet. If it is already installed, skip this: the example starts from opening a file.

Before you start

  • Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), or Linux x86_64 with a desktop. Ubuntu 22.04 and 24.04 are the systems the Linux package has been checked on. macOS is not supported.
  • Nothing else to install: no Python, Node.js or Rust. There is no account.

Steps

  1. Open Studio's Releases page (opens in a new tab) and download the Windows installer (Snapmaker.Studio_<version>_x64-setup.exe) or the Linux .deb. Download only from that page.
  2. Check the download against the SHA256 hash (a fingerprint that shows the file is intact) in RELEASE_METADATA.md (opens in a new tab); that file is the one place current hashes are kept.
    • Windows (PowerShell): Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256 .\<installer>.exe
    • Linux: sha256sum <file>.deb
    • If the values differ, delete the file and download it again.
  3. Windows: run the installer. It is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may say Unknown publisher. Once the hash matches, choose More info → Run anyway, then start Snapmaker Studio from the Start menu. More: Windows install guide (opens in a new tab).
  4. Linux: sudo apt install ./<the .deb file name exactly as shown on the Releases page>, then start Snapmaker Studio from your application menu or run snapmaker-studio-desktop. More: Linux install guide (opens in a new tab).

You are done when

  • Studio opens on Home, with the version v1.5.1 in the status bar and Local-only · nothing leaves your network in the footer.
  • There is nothing to sign in to.

If something is different

Windows says “Windows protected your PC”.

This is expected for an installer that is not code-signed yet. Continue only if the SHA256 matches and you downloaded the file from Studio's Releases page.

The hash does not match.

Do not run the installer. Delete it and download it again from Studio's Releases page.

Studio looks different from this guide.

This guide describes v1.5.1. Check the version at the bottom of the Studio window; the release notes (opens in a new tab) say what changed.

Limit

Linux support is verified by automated installs on clean Ubuntu 22.04 and 24.04 systems. Other distributions and installs on a physical desktop have not been verified yet; see the Linux install guide (opens in a new tab).

Safety

Uninstalling does not delete your project data. On Windows it lives in %LOCALAPPDATA%\SnapmakerStudio.

Check colors against the four toolheads

Use this when: Your project has several colors and you want to know whether the U1 can print them.

Before you start

  • A multicolor project open, such as demo_u1_showcase.3mf from the examples (opens in a new tab).

Steps

  1. Open Colors & Materials (Advanced sidebar, or More tools).
  2. Read Colours and toolheads: the example says 6 colours, 4 toolheads — possible without repainting.
  3. Read the groups: Not proven separable — reserve a toolhead each, Appear only higher up — could be a planned swap, Studio can’t tell how these are used.
  4. For a painted project, a box says how many filament colors the painting uses; expand What Studio read from the painting for measurements.

You are done when

  • A plain sentence comparing your colors with the four toolheads, and the line Load the colours in the order the project lists them.

If something is different

Colors are listed under “Studio can’t tell how these are used”.

The project does not say which object, painted region or color change uses that slot. Load them in the order the project lists, and check in Orca.

Studio can’t tell

The number four comes from the U1's published specification (four toolheads). Studio says it did not read this from your printer.

Important

Assigning a color in a file is not loading filament. Putting filament in a toolhead is a physical job only you do.

Move an object that hangs off the plate

Use this when: Object placement says an object is outside the U1's printable area.

Before you start

  • The project open in Studio.

Steps

  1. Find Object placement (Compatibility, This print or the Workspace): Hangs 55.0 mm past the right edge.
  2. If it offers Move onto the plate (saves a copy), press it. If there is no such button, see the placement example in the guide.
  3. The card turns green: 1 object(s) moved onto the U1 plate in a new copy — demo_offplate_foreign_placed_U1.3mf. Your original file was not changed.
  4. Open that copy (Open a model) and prepare it (Prepare U1 copy).

You are done when

  • A new …_placed_U1.3mf beside your original, and the card says The object sits inside the U1's printable area.

If something is different

There is no Move button.

Either every object already fits, or one move cannot fix the layout (Studio also never moves multi-plate projects). Open the project in Orca and use Arrange; see the placement example.

Safety

The moved copy shifts the whole layout as one piece; the spacing between objects, their rotation, scale and height stay as the creator set them.

Choose spools and Orca presets (Project Materials)

Use this when: You track spools and want each color tied to a real spool and an installed Orca preset. New in v1.5.0.

Before you start

  • Optional: a spool provider (such as Spoolman) set up in Settings → Materials provider. Without one, you can still choose presets.

Steps

  1. On Compatibility, find Project materials. For each slot Studio lists up to three spools with reasons. No spool is selected for you.
  2. Select the spool in your hand, then press Use this preset (the suggestion), Choose an installed preset, or Keep project's filament.
  3. Press Review & prepare, read the review, then Prepare with these choices.
  4. Select Remember this mapping to reuse it. Studio saves it after the copy is written and warns you if saving fails. A saved mapping is checked again each time it is used.

You are done when

  • The review lists, per slot, what will be written; the new copy has the spool's color and the preset name.

If something is different

There are no spool suggestions.

No provider is set up, or none matches the material. You can still choose an installed preset or keep the project's filament.

Prepare stopped appears.

Two slots share one preset but the project declares different vendors or types for them. Choose a different preset for one slot.

Studio can’t tell

Orca's own installed presets can be checked against the U1 and are shown as Proven. Presets you made yourself are shown as Confirmed by you and Manual check in Orca required: Studio cannot confirm Orca applies their temperature, flow and cooling.

Limit

Project Materials does not create custom Orca presets, choose a spool for you, or write to your spool provider.

What to review in Orca before slicing

Use this when: The copy is open in Orca and you are about to slice.

Before you start

  • The prepared copy open in Orca.

Steps

  1. Check that the printer is Snapmaker U1.
  2. Check that the filaments and colors match what you will load, toolhead by toolhead.
  3. Check that every object is on the plate. Use Arrange if unsure; Studio does not verify spacing between objects.
  4. Check that supports are set the way you want. Studio's fix plan marks these as Do this in Orca.
  5. If a preset you made is named in the copy, check its values in Orca (Manual check in Orca required).
  6. Slice (Slice plate), then export the G-code with Orca's own export command.

You are done when

  • You have an exported .gcode file and know what you checked.

If something is different

Orca shows a “Customized Preset” notice.

That can happen when the creator's tuned settings are kept. Review the settings before slicing.

Limit

Orca's own screens are not shown in this guide, and its menu wording can differ between versions.

Connect your printer

Use this when: You want live status and comparisons against your actual U1.

Before you start

  • Your U1 is on and connected to the same network as this computer.
  • On the U1's touchscreen, Advanced Mode is on under Settings → Maintenance; Studio's own message says the printer's network interface opens only after you turn Advanced Mode on.

Steps

  1. Open Printer (Simple) or Printer Hub (Advanced).
  2. Under Connect to your U1, type the printer's network name (the hint in the field is U1.local) or IP address and press Connect, or press Auto-detect my U1.
  3. Connecting saves the address. You can also change it under Settings → Printer, in the field whose hint reads Network name or IP.
  4. Read what Printer Hub reports. Anything the printer does not report is marked as such, for example Nozzle sizes: not reported.

You are done when

  • When the printer answers, Printer Hub shows its live status and the controls become available. Otherwise: Controls are off until the printer is connected and reachable.

If something is different

Printer Hub says “Printer not found”.

Studio could not get a usable answer at that address. Check that the printer is on and on this network, that the address is right, and that Advanced Mode is on under Settings → Maintenance on the touchscreen.

Auto-detect finds nothing.

Type the name or IP address and press Connect.

Safety

Monitoring is read-only. Studio never starts a print by itself.

Limit

Use the printer controls safely

Use this when: A job is checked and you are at the printer. Studio never starts a print by itself.

Before you start

  • The printer is connected in Printer Hub (see Connect your printer).

Steps

  1. Pause and Resume are sent to the printer as soon as you press them, with no second prompt; both are reversible. Watch the printer's status in Printer Hub: a request can fail.
  2. Cancel print asks first: Cancel this print? A cancelled print cannot be resumed.
  3. Upload sliced gcode: you choose the file. Start this print then asks you to confirm, naming the uploaded file; Studio does not check the bed for you.
  4. Emergency stop asks first, then sends the printer an emergency-stop command. Studio does not confirm that motion and heaters have stopped; a firmware restart may be needed before printing again.

You are done when

  • Nothing is sent to the printer until you press the relevant button and, when Studio asks, confirm the action.

If something is different

The controls are grayed out.

They are off until the printer is connected and reachable. That is deliberate.

Safety

Load the filament and check the bed yourself before starting. Studio does not inspect them.

What it means

The sliced job names a printer other than the U1, or a printer name Studio does not recognize as the U1. Toolpaths, temperatures and machine commands are generated for one specific machine.

Why you see this

Studio reads the printer name in the G-code and does not need a printer connected to do so. A job made for another printer may contain commands or settings that do not suit the U1, so re-slice it with a U1 profile.

What to do

  1. Re-slice the model in Snapmaker Orca with a U1 profile, and check the new job.

What not to conclude

Do not send the job anyway. When the name is one Studio does not recognize, the mismatch is likely rather than proven: find out in your slicer which printer profile made the file, and re-slice unless it really was a U1 profile.
